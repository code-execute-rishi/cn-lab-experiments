LAB 11: 1-PERSISTENT & P-PERSISTENT CSMA/CD WITH BINARY EXPONENTIAL BACKOFF
===========================================================================

QUESTION
--------
Aim: To design, simulate, and comparatively evaluate 1-Persistent and p-Persistent CSMA/CD incorporating real-time collision sensing, 32-bit jamming signal abortion, and the IEEE 802.3 Truncated Binary Exponential Backoff (BEB) algorithm in Python.

FILES
-----
- csma_cd_protocols.py: 1-persistent and p-persistent CSMA/CD with truncated BEB under light, medium and heavy load.

LOGIC (HOW THE PROGRAM WORKS)
-----------------------------
csma_cd_protocols.py
1. Setup: 8 stations, time in slots of 51.2 us, 1000 slots. A frame takes 2 slots; a collision is detected within 1 slot.
2. Each slot, every station gets a new frame with a given probability (light 2%, medium 6%, heavy 12%). Backoff counters go down by 1.
3. If the channel is busy, nobody starts. Otherwise, each station whose backoff is 0:
   - 1-persistent: transmits at once;
   - p-persistent: transmits with probability p, otherwise waits 1 slot.
4. 0 stations -> idle slot. 1 station -> success: the channel is busy for 2 slots and the delay is recorded.
5. 2 or more -> collision: detected in 1 slot and the jam signal is sent. For each collided frame:
   collisions + 1; after 16 attempts the frame is dropped; otherwise Binary Exponential Backoff:
   k = min(collisions, 10), wait a random 0 .. 2^k - 1 slots.
6. Throughput = delivered x 2 / total slots. The table compares 1-persistent, p = 0.5 and p = 0.2 at the three loads.

HOW TO RUN
----------
No input is needed; the test data is built into each program.
python3 csma_cd_protocols.py

VIVA QUESTIONS
--------------
Q1. What is CSMA/CD?
A:  CSMA with Collision Detection: a station keeps listening while it transmits and stops immediately if it detects a collision. Used by classic Ethernet.

Q2. Why send a jam signal?
A:  The 32-bit jam signal makes sure every station notices the collision.

Q3. What is Binary Exponential Backoff?
A:  After the k-th collision, wait a random number of slots between 0 and 2^k − 1. k stops growing at 10, and the frame is dropped after 16 attempts.

Q4. Why is there a minimum Ethernet frame size (64 bytes)?
A:  The sender must still be transmitting when a collision from the far end comes back: frame time ≥ 2 × propagation delay.

Q5. 1-persistent vs p-persistent?
A:  1-persistent sends as soon as the channel is idle (many collisions under load). p-persistent sends with probability p, reducing collisions but adding delay.

Q6. Why is CSMA/CD not used in Wi-Fi?
A:  A wireless station cannot hear collisions while it transmits. Wi-Fi uses CSMA/CA (collision avoidance) instead.

Q7. What is the slot time in 10 Mbps Ethernet?
A:  51.2 µs, the time to send 512 bits.

Q8. CSMA/CD vs CSMA/CA?
A:  CD detects collisions and stops (wired Ethernet). CA tries to avoid them with random waits and RTS/CTS (wireless).
